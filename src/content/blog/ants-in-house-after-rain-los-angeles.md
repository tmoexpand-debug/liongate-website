A dry stretch of November in Los Angeles can end on a wet weekend. You go to bed with a quiet kitchen and wake up to a line of ants along the counter, the sink, or the bathroom floor. Ants in the house after rain is a late-fall pattern in the city and the San Fernando Valley.

The ants in that line are usually Argentine ants, the small dark ones that travel in a trail. They nest in soil, mulch, and potted plants around the house. A storm fills those nests with water. The colony shifts toward whatever is still dry, and the house is the driest thing on the lot.

## Why the first rains push them indoors

Argentine ants stay active through a Southern California winter. They do not shut down when the nights cool off. What changes in late November and early December is the ground.

**The nest gets wet.** Many of these colonies sit in shallow soil, under a layer of mulch, or inside an irrigation or meter box. The first real winter rain can fill that space. Workers move up and out. A foundation wall, a gap beside a pipe, and the cabinet under the sink are dry, so the trail turns inward.

**They were often already at the wall.** Through the warm months the same ants may have stayed outside, running the slab, the weep screed, or a hose bib. Rain does not open a new hole. It sends them through the gap they were already using.

**Water and food are both inside.** A pet bowl left out, a drip under the sink, or a sticky patch on the counter is enough to hold them. Bathrooms show up in these calls because the ants are after moisture, not only sweets.

A lot in Sherman Oaks, Van Nuys, or Burbank can go from a dry slab to standing water overnight. Neighborhoods closer to the coast stay damper all year, so the indoor rush is sometimes smaller, but a soaking rain still moves yard ants. Not every house gets a trail. The ones that do usually have mulch or plants against the stucco and a gap where a pipe or the slab meets the wall.

Heat is the other half of the year. In summer the same ants come inside looking for water, which is the note on our [common pests](/common-pests/) page. Late fall is the rain version of that move.

## Where you see them the morning after

Walk the house before you wipe the trail. The path tells you which side of the building they are using.

- **Kitchen edges.** The back of the counter, the sink rim, and the seam where the cabinet meets the wall. A line that disappears under the sink is coming from the wall, not from a crumb.
- **Bathrooms.** The floor by the toilet, the tub edge, and the vanity. If the trail is only there, check for a drip, then look outside on that same wall.
- **Doors and windows.** Sliding-door tracks and window sills hold water after a storm, then ants step inside where the sweep is worn.
- **The garage.** The joint where the slab meets the driveway, and the corner by the water heater. A trail there often matches mulch or a valve box just outside.
- **Outlets and pipe holes.** Ants crossing an outlet cover or a cable opening are in a void. Note the room, then inspect that wall outside.

Outside, walk the foundation while the soil is still wet. Mulch piled against the stucco, a pot sitting in a saucer, and a meter or irrigation box are the places a shallow nest was sitting before the rain. If the indoor trail lines up with one of those, you have found the side that matters.

## What is worth doing before the next storm

1. **Wipe the line you can see.** A soapy cloth removes the trail on the counter and the baseboard. They may lay it down again. Wiping shows you whether they return to the same spot.
2. **Take away the easy meal and the drip.** Bring pet bowls in overnight. Wipe grease and sugar. Catch a faucet that drips into the cabinet, and dry the saucer under a houseplant.
3. **Pull cover off the foundation.** Rake mulch back so it does not touch the stucco. Lift pots off the soil. A nest pressed against the house is the one a storm pushes inside first.
4. **Close the obvious gaps.** Look where a pipe or a cable enters under the sink and along the garage wall. A crack you can see at the slab, or a door sweep that leaves a slit of light, is a path. Caulk and a new sweep do not replace finding the nest.
5. **Do not stop at the ants on the counter.** That line is workers. Spraying it does not reach a nest in the yard. Note where they enter, then deal with the outside on that wall.

If the trail is gone in a day and stays gone after the soil drains, the colony may have moved on. If it comes back with the next rain, the nest is still beside the house.

## When the trail keeps coming back

One storm can be a passing line. A trail after every rain, or ants in more than one room, usually means the house is on that colony's route. Wiping the counter will not move a nest in the bed along the foundation.
