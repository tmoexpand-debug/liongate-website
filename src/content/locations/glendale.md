## Roof rats on the foothill edge

Above a Glendale ceiling, the rat is usually a roof rat: a dark, slim climber with a tail longer than its body. Norway rats are heavier and stay nearer the ground. Roof rats use fences, palms, oak limbs, and fruit trees. The short routes here are citrus on the roof, ivy on a block wall, and brush where a lot meets the slope.

Verdugo Woodlands, Glenoaks Canyon, and the streets above Montrose sit on that Verdugo edge. Chevy Chase Canyon does the same farther east, where decks sit over the hill. Kenneth Village, in northwest Glendale near the Burbank line, more often has a tree or a utility line as the bridge. This is where we look first when someone hears running after dark. It is not evidence that every house has rats.

The clues usually come first: scratching overhead in the evening, hollowed fruit, droppings on a garage shelf, and dark rub marks along a pipe. A kitchen trap rarely ends it, because the nest is often above the ceiling. The visit has to look for openings at the eaves and vents, and for limbs on the roof. ## Ants, spiders, and termites

Argentine ants are the small dark ants that trail along foundations, sinks, and pet bowls. They are the main nuisance ant in urban Southern California, Glendale included. When a yard dries out they follow water — irrigation on a canyon lot, a slab edge, a weep screed, or a pipe entry. Courtyard drains do the same job downtown.

Black widows are a common garage spider in this part of Los Angeles County. They sit in stored boxes, under decks, in block-wall weep holes, and in closed sheds. A bite is a medical concern.

Drywood termites live in the wood they eat. Look for hard pellets under a pinpoint hole in a beam, window, fence, or garage header. Rossmoyne, Adams Hill, and Verdugo Woodlands have many older wood-frame houses, so name those pellets when you call. On a slope, soil against the foundation is where subterranean termites build mud tubes. They are a different insect. A general pest visit is not a termite inspection. If you find pellets, wings, or tubes, say so. We identify what is there before recommending any work. See [drywood vs. subterranean termites](/blog/drywood-vs-subterranean-termites/).

German cockroaches show up in apartments and can move between kitchens on shared plumbing. That fits Brand Boulevard more than a house in the hills. Describe the room and the time of day if you are not sure which pest it is.

## Neighborhoods

Montrose is north Glendale, around the Honolulu Avenue shops, toward the Verdugo and San Gabriel foothills. Fruit trees, ivy, and fence-line brush are the first questions, including in neighboring Sparr Heights.

Verdugo Woodlands is oaks and larger lots between the Verdugo Mountains and the San Rafael Hills. Limbs over the roof are a rodent path. Older eaves are where pellets show up.

Rossmoyne, northeast of downtown, is older houses with wood trim, attics, and detached garages. Ant trails at the foundation and drywood pellets are what people usually describe.

Adams Hill is the southeast hillside near the Los Angeles line. Decks and bungalows mean spiders underneath, rodents from the slope, and termites in the framing.

Glenoaks Canyon puts brush next to the house, so the trip to the roof is short. Say if the lot backs onto the hill.

Kenneth Village runs along Kenneth Road in northwest Glendale, toward Brand Park and the Verdugo base, on the line with Burbank. If the address is on the Burbank side, see [Burbank pest control](/pest-control/burbank/) and call to confirm the route.

Chevy Chase Canyon is eastern Glendale: winding roads and decks over slope. A canyon address tells us what to inspect. It is not proof of an infestation.

Downtown Glendale is Brand Boulevard and the apartments around it. Those calls are often ants or cockroaches on a shared wall. If your street is not listed, call (818) 353-4800 with the cross streets.
